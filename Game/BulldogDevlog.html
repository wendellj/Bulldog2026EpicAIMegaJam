Bulldogs Directors Cut - Development Log

2026 Epic AI MegaJam Entry Team: Bellevue College Bulldogs Platform: Meta Quest 3 (Android) Engine: Unreal Engine 5.8

A Note to the Judges

We are students from the Bellevue College Digital Media Arts program, using hardware on loan from the Bellevue College XR Lab. To our knowledge, we are the only team in this jam targeting Unreal Engine VR for Meta Quest 3.

Yes, we chose hard mode.

Please try this on an actual Quest 3 headset. This is a roomscale VR experience built around hand tracking, physical movement, and presence. You cannot judge a VR game by watching a video or playing on a flatscreen any more than you can judge a roller coaster by watching a video. This game was designed for VR from the ground up, not adapted for it.

We built for actual VR roomscale, not "standing in one spot wiggling your arms like you're directing traffic." Almost every VR game on the market was built for tethered headsets. Stand in one place, wave at things. Maybe teleport.

With our game, you walk across a bridge, sidestep a boulder, dodge a car in traffic. Your play space is the game space, your legs are the locomotion system.

Roomscale VR is what all the cyberpunk novels promised us in the 90s. Gibson, Stephenson, the whole crew. They imagined jacking into cyberspace and being there. Now we're actually doing it, and most developers are still making flat games with a headset strapped on. This isn't Ready Player One yet, but it's closer than most people realize.

The concept started with a simple thought: Directors Cut. XR movie scenes where players do the actor's role. You don't watch the scene. You are the scene.

The first idea that came to mind was a bit of film trivia: Alan Rickman insisted on performing the free-fall stunt from Nakatomi Plaza in Die Hard himself. That split-second of genuine surprise in his expression? The stunt coordinator told him he'd drop on three. He dropped on one. Rickman's real shock became cinema history.

So we asked ourselves: could we, in roomscale VR, give someone the sensation of falling off the top of a skyscraper? Could we make their stomach drop? Could we make them grab for a railing that isn't there?

Then we started throwing around other action movie tropes. Someone pitched "The Falling Debris Weave": During a building collapse, rockslide, or crumbling bridge, the actor has to sidestep falling boulders or leap across widening gaps as the ground gives way beneath them. That became CliffPath. The rickety rope bridge over a canyon? Every Indiana Jones movie ever made. Low-gravity car hopping? We honestly don't know where that came from, but it felt right at 2am.

That's what we built. That's why we're here.

And hey, if you're a judge without VR hardware, that's not your fault. Epic has poured resources into Fortnite, UEFN, films, TV, and even that weird MetaHuman thing, but VR support in Unreal remains an afterthought. If they wanted you to fairly evaluate VR entries, they would have shipped you a Quest 3 along with your judging assignment. They didn't. So here we are: we built a VR game for a jam that doesn't guarantee VR-capable judges. Bold? Maybe. Stupid? Possibly. But we'd rather swing for the fences than build another puzzle platformer.

We were all working remotely, scattered across the Seattle area, coordinating through Discord and Git like a startup that can't afford an office. Our Discord logs tell the story: standup messages at 7am, debug sessions at 11pm, Rob posting at 3:35am about Quest deployment while Wendell replied at 3:38am. Brian pushed level layouts at 1:23am. Jacob delivered audio assets at 2am. Nobody was in the same room. Nobody had the same sleep schedule. But the commits kept coming.

Thanks for reading. Now put on the headset.

What You're Getting Into

Bulldogs Directors Cut is a VR experience inspired by iconic movie scenes. No controllers. Just your hands, your body, and your ability to not panic when your brain thinks you're about to die.

TheFall — Hans Gruber falling off Nakatomi Plaza, but make it a whole level. You're plummeting down the side of the building, the Los Angeles skyline spinning around you, the ground rushing up to meet you. It's basically a controlled panic attack. You're welcome.

CliffPath — Remember that scene in every action movie where the hero inches along a crumbling cliff while rocks fall around them? That's you now. Dodge boulders by actually moving your body. Duck. Lean. Sidestep. Your couch won't save you here. Rob built this level after his mom went to the ICU mid-jam, and he still delivered. If he can do that, you can dodge some rocks.

CanyonBridge — Indiana Jones. Temple of Doom. That rope bridge. You know the one. Our teammate ln37 modeled the planks in Blender, and Claude (yes, the AI) placed them one by one in Unreal. Walk across. Look down at the canyon floor a hundred meters below. Try to remember that none of this is real. Your legs won't believe you.

Highway — Frogger, but you're the frog. Stand on the shoulder, wait for a gap, sprint across four lanes of traffic before something flattens you. Brian started the layout, Rob finished it with cars, audio, and the logic that makes you feel genuinely nervous about crossing the street.

CarHop — Low gravity parking lot. Cars scattered like stepping stones. Bob your head to jump. Steer with your hands mid-air like you're conducting an orchestra of bad decisions. Wendell built this at 2am while debugging hand tracking for the fifth time that night.

Installation Guide (Sideloading the APK)

This game is not on the Meta Store. You'll need to "sideload" it, which just means installing an app from outside the store. It takes about 5 minutes.

What You Need

  • Meta Quest 3 headset (Quest 2 may work but is untested)
  • USB-C cable that actually transfers data, not just the garbage charging-only cable that came with your vape. If your computer doesn't see the headset, it's the cable. It's always the cable.
  • A computer (Windows, Mac, or Linux)
  • A Meta account with Developer Mode enabled

Step 1: Enable Developer Mode

  1. Install the Meta Quest app on your phone (iOS or Android)
  2. Open the app and sign in with your Meta account
  3. Go to Menu → Devices → Select your Quest 3
  4. Tap Headset Settings → Developer Mode → Enable
  5. If prompted to register as a developer, go to developer.oculus.com and create an organization (free, takes 30 seconds)

Step 2: Install SideQuest (Easiest Method)

  1. Download SideQuest Advanced Installer from sidequestvr.com
  2. Install and open SideQuest
  3. Connect your Quest 3 via USB-C
  4. Put on the headset and click Allow when prompted to trust the computer
  5. In SideQuest, click the Install APK button (folder icon with down arrow)
  6. Select BulldogMegaJam-Android-Shipping.apk from the zip
  7. Wait for "APK installed successfully"

Step 3: Launch the Game

  1. Put on your Quest 3 headset
  2. Go to App Library (grid icon in the main menu)
  3. In the top-right dropdown, select Unknown Sources
  4. Find Bulldogs Directors Cut and click to launch

Alternative: ADB Command Line

If you prefer command line:

adb install -r BulldogMegaJam-Android-Shipping.apk

First Time in VR? Read This First

If you've never used a Quest 3 before, here's what to expect.

Physical Setup

  1. Clear your play space. You need at least 3m x 3m (10ft x 10ft) of open floor. Move furniture, warn roommates, lock up pets.

  2. Set your Guardian boundary. When you put on the headset, Quest will ask you to trace the edges of your play area. Do this carefully. The game uses your full space.

  3. Adjust the headset. If Epic got you the base model Quest 3, you'll be fumbling with a flimsy fabric strap like it's 2016. Our condolences. But if they sprung for a BoboVR or Elite strap, just tighten the dial on the back like you're putting on a high-end hardhat. IPD (eye spacing) is adjusted by physically sliding the lenses left/right. If things look blurry, adjust until sharp.

Hand Tracking (No Controllers)

This game uses hand tracking only. Put your Touch controllers down, you won't need them.

  • Hold your hands in front of you where the headset cameras can see them
  • Good lighting helps tracking accuracy
  • Don't wear gloves
  • If hands disappear, bring them back into view in front of your face

Playing the Levels

  • TheFall: You're falling down the side of Nakatomi Plaza. Look around at the LA skyline as you plummet. Try not to think about the ground.

  • CliffPath: You're on a narrow mountain ledge. Dodge falling rocks by physically ducking, leaning, and stepping aside. Yes, move your actual body.

  • CanyonBridge: Walk across a rickety rope bridge. Look down if you dare. Place your feet carefully on the slats.

  • Highway: Frogger-style highway crossing. Wait for gaps in traffic, then sprint across. Don't get hit.

  • CarHop: Low gravity car hopping. Bob your head up quickly to jump. Use your hands to steer in the air.

If You Feel Sick

VR can cause motion sickness, especially for first-timers. If you feel queasy: - Stop immediately and remove the headset - Focus on a stationary object in the real world - Get some fresh air - Try again later in shorter sessions

The CliffPath level involves looking at heights. If you have a fear of heights, it will feel real. That's the point, but take breaks if needed.

Returning to the Main Menu

There are no buttons or menus to hunt for. You leave a level the same way you entered it: with your hands.

  1. Hold both hands up in front of you, palms facing each other, about arm's length from your chest.
  2. Press your palms together, the way you would when clapping once and stopping. Press them flat against each other, don't hover.
  3. Keep them pressed together for one second (roughly the length of saying "one Mississippi").
  4. The screen fills with DIRECTOR'S CUT for three seconds, then you're back at the menu, standing in front of the level panels again.

Things to know:

  • One second is a hold, not a tap. If you pull your hands apart too early, nothing happens and you can simply start again. There's no penalty for a missed gesture.
  • It only works with both hands. One hand alone will not do it.
  • The gesture is off inside the menu itself, so you can't accidentally reload the menu while standing in it.
  • If nothing happens, check that your headset can still see both hands. In dim light Quest tracking drops out, and the gesture can't fire if your hands aren't being tracked. Bring them up into view for a moment and try again.
  • Don't try this while falling. The Fall ends on its own and takes you back to the menu automatically.

for i in range(10):
    actor('spawn_from_class',
          class_path='/Script/Engine.StaticMeshActor',
          location=[0, -200 + i*45, 0])

Capabilities used: - actor('spawn_from_class') - Spawn actors - actor('delete_by_label') - Remove actors - actor('set_component_property') - Configure components - material() - Create and edit materials

GitHub Issues Filed: We ran GenOrca hard enough to find its edges. Wendell filed four issues on the GenOrca/unreal-mcp repo during the jam:

  • #69 - MetaXR plugin components not discoverable (ISDK hand tracking components invisible to MCP)
  • #72 - Component bound events (K2Node_ComponentBoundEvent) can't be wired via MCP
  • #73 - Object reference pins (Sound, Material assets) can't be set programmatically
  • #74 - BreakStruct nodes (K2Node_BreakStruct) not supported

When your AI tooling breaks, you file bugs upstream instead of complaining. That's how open source works. zenoengine has been responsive and the project is actively maintained (147 stars, last commit July 2026).

Technical Challenges & Solutions

Challenge 1: Hand Tracking Not Working

Problem: Hands would not stay attached to player when walking around. Controllers worked but hands were stuck in place.

Solution: Migrated from OculusXRHandComponent to Meta Interaction SDK (ISDK). Created BP_VRPawn_ISDK with IsdkHandRigComponentLeft/Right properly attached to MotionControllers.

Commit: 12beda8 - "feat: Fix VR hand tracking with Meta Interaction SDK (ISDK)"

Challenge 2: Meta XR Plugins Not Compatible with UE 5.9

Problem: Team member Rob upgraded to UE 5.9.0 but Meta XR plugins only support up to 5.8.x.

Solution: Stayed on UE 5.8.4. Rules clarify 5.9.0 is only required for web builds (experimental).

Discord: "We're technically an Android build, so UE 5.8 is explicitly allowed"

Challenge 3: FBX Import Crashes Editor

Problem: Importing FBX via Python (AssetImportTask) caused TaskGraph assertion failures.

Error: Assertion failed: ++Queue(QueueIndex).RecursionGuard == 1

Workaround: Import FBX manually via Content Browser instead of programmatically.

Challenge 4: GenOrca Cannot Set Object References

Problem: set_component_property cannot set meshes, materials, or textures.

Workaround: Use exec_python() with direct Python API calls:

exec_python("""
import unreal
mesh = unreal.EditorAssetLibrary.load_asset('/Game/Path/To/Mesh')
comp.set_static_mesh(mesh)
""")

Challenge 5: Level Audio Starts Before Player Ready

Problem: BeginPlay fires before VR presentation is ready, so intro audio plays during loading.

Solution: Start audio from a short delay instead of BeginPlay, so clock and player perception begin together.

Lessons Learned

  1. AI can't test VR - Claude created levels but couldn't validate motion/physics feel. Human testing on Quest 3 was essential.

  2. MCP + GenOrca = powerful - Direct editor control saved hours of manual clicking. 191 Unreal actions available via Python.

  3. Reference images help AI - Rob found providing a photo reference dramatically improved Claude's level generation.

  4. Family comes first - Rob's mom went to ICU mid-jam. Team supported him, he still delivered two levels.

  5. Plugin compatibility matters - Check engine version support before upgrading. Meta XR doesn't support 5.9.0.
